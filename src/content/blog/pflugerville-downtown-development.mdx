Pflugerville downtown development is a $223 million project to build a downtown the city has never had. It's 29 acres at the northwest corner of Pecan Street and FM 685, and as of November 2026 the city says it will be substantially complete on December 18, with the new City Hall opening to the public on January 4, 2027.

That's the centerpiece, but it isn't the only big public build. The city is also putting about $187 million into its water plant and $130 million into a second raw water line, and Spartnerships put the total of major infrastructure projects at more than $548 million in its September 2026 roundup.

I'm an Austin real estate agent with eXp Realty and I list homes across the metro, including in Pflugerville, where I live. I've driven past this site on Pecan Street for three years and watched it come out of the dirt. Below is what's actually being built, who's paying for it, what's still unconfirmed, and what it could mean if you own a home nearby. ## Downtown East: the $223M centerpiece

Downtown East is 29 acres at the northwest corner of Pecan Street and FM 685, right across from the old downtown. If you live in Falcon Pointe or anywhere off Pecan, you've seen the cranes.

Here's what's going in:

- A new City Hall and a large recreation center
- A public plaza with a walking promenade
- Some retail space and a parking garage
- Main Street, extended through the middle of the site
- A connection to the city's hike and bike trail

The city owns the project and Griffin Swinerton is the builder. It's funded by voter-approved bonds, certificates of obligation, money from the Pflugerville Community Development Corporation, and a tax increment reinvestment zone, or TIRZ. In plain terms, the land in that zone pays a certain amount of property tax today. As the area builds up and the land is worth more, the extra tax money goes back into paying for the project instead of into the general pot. Both the $223 million figure and the funding mix come from Statesman coverage, which a Yahoo repost of the "4 major Pflugerville projects" story also reports.

The timeline, per the city's September 22 council presentation (as reported by [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/pflugerville-hutto/government/pflugerville-s-downtown-east-nears-completion/)):

- Substantial completion: December 18, 2026
- City Hall opens to the public: January 4, 2027
- Recreation center soft opening: mid-January 2027
- Grand celebration: April 2027

I want to be straight about two things. The project has said "late 2026" since it broke ground in 2023, and so far it has held to that. But the city did have to add a parking garage after the fact. And the only private tenant I can confirm is a coffee and beer cafe inside City Hall, so I'm not promising anyone a street full of restaurants yet.

## City Hall: $83.9M, with a cafe on the ground floor

The new City Hall costs $83.9 million. It's about 107,000 square feet and brings 11 city departments under one roof, plus the council chambers and the municipal court. The ground floor has a public cafe, West Pecan Coffee and Beer, whose lease runs through January 1, 2030.

Both City Hall and The Monarch are inside the $223 million, not on top of it. If you see the numbers added together somewhere, that's double counting.

For homeowners nearby, the cafe is the interesting part. It's an early sign the city wants this to be a place people hang out, not only a place to pay a ticket.

## The Monarch: an $81.7M recreation center

The Monarch recreation center costs $81.7 million. Published reports put it at more than 100,000 square feet. It includes four convertible, high school size basketball courts, an indoor turf field, and an indoor pool with a lazy river and water slides.

One heads-up: the current recreation center is scheduled to close January 10, and The Monarch soft opens in mid-January, with a ribbon cutting expected in January or February. Plan for a short gap if your family uses the old one.

When a family is choosing between Pflugerville and [Round Rock](/blog/round-rock-development-projects/), "what is there to do here" is a real question. For a long time, Pflugerville didn't have a great answer. A new recreation center with an indoor pool is a concrete answer.

## The water plant and the second raw water line: $187M and $130M

This is the part nobody puts on a postcard, and it's the biggest check after Downtown East.

**Surface water treatment plant.** The city is spending about $187 million to expand the plant from 17.7 million gallons a day to 30 million. The city says that covers projected demand through 2040. Completion is expected around January 2027. The cost has grown over time, so if you saw a smaller number a while back, that's why.

**Secondary raw water line.** This is a 15-mile, 42-inch pipe from the Colorado River to Lake Pflugerville, running parallel to the existing 30-inch line. It costs about $130 million, and delivery is expected in December 2026. Think of it as a second straw: with two lines side by side, one problem doesn't shut off the whole city.

Both are paid for with municipal bonds and Texas Water Development Board loans. Some of those bonds were issued without a public vote, and they're repaid through utility rates and taxes. I'm not predicting what that does to your bill, but you should know it's coming from somewhere. For more on how local taxes work, see my [Austin property tax guide](/blog/austin-property-tax-guide-2026/).

Why does a water plant matter to a homeowner? A city can only grow as fast as its water lets it. Every new subdivision east of SH 130 depends on this kind of capacity.

## Baylor Scott & White: the hospital doubled its beds

Baylor Scott & White Medical Center, Pflugerville sits at 2600 E Pflugerville Parkway, right by SH 130. Its expansion took it from 25 beds to 50, added emergency room capacity and roughly quadrupled the clinic space, per [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/pflugerville-hutto/healthcare/baylor-scott-white-pflugerville-celebrates-hospital-expansion/). The celebration was August 13, 2025. I couldn't verify a final construction cost, so I'm not quoting one.

"How far is the nearest hospital" quietly shows up in a lot of buyer decisions, especially for families raising kids or caring for parents. A bigger hospital near SH 130 is one less objection a buyer has to talk themselves past.

## Parks: about $19M opening in 2026

Per [Community Impact](https://communityimpact.com/austin/pflugerville-hutto/development/2026/01/16/19m-in-pflugerville-park-upgrades-to-debut-in-2026/), about $19 million in park projects were set to debut in 2026, part of a 2020 bond of $42.3 million.

- **Kelly Lane Park** ($2.1 million) added the city's first public pickleball courts.
- **1849 Park** is adding baseball and softball fields on a 300-acre site.
- **Lake Pflugerville Park Phase 2** ($10.6 million) is further out, with construction running from September 2027 to September 2028.

The city calls itself the Trail Capital of Texas, with about 60 miles of trails. Once Downtown East plugs into that system, a lot of neighborhoods will be able to bike straight to the new downtown.

## The Colored Addition: $8.43M for five streets

Near old downtown is a neighborhood called the Colored Addition. Black residents founded it in 1910 because they weren't allowed to live inside the city limits at the time, and for a long time its streets didn't get the same investment as the rest of the city.

Voters approved $8.43 million to rebuild five of those streets: Russell, Caldwells Lane, Taylor, Lincoln and Grant. The work is scheduled to finish in July 2027. It's a neighborhood that waited a long time for this, and it's worth saying out loud.

## FM 685 widening: planned, not funded

This is the one I'd be careful with. There's a plan to widen FM 685 to three lanes each way between SH 130 and Wells Branch Parkway, with a continuous-flow intersection at Pflugerville Parkway. The cost attached to it is up to $126.7 million.

When Community Impact reported on it in March 2023, no funding source had been identified for construction. As of November 2026 I'm treating it as a plan, not a project under construction. ## Pflugerville Crossing: the $600M project that disappeared

Before any of this, there was a project called Pflugerville Crossing, which some people knew as Sunshine Village. It was about 120 acres at SH 130 and FM 685, next to Hawaiian Falls.

The 2020 pitch was huge: more than $600 million, over 400,000 square feet of retail, hotels and 22 restaurants. The city signed a Chapter 380 agreement to rebate 75 percent of the sales and property tax the project generated, up to about $303 million over 30 years.

Then it fell apart. The agreement was terminated, the city says it no longer exists, and lawsuits followed, according to [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/lawsuit-follows-termination-of-major-pflugerville-development/). Nothing I've found says it's coming back, and I wouldn't plan around it.

The takeaway: most of what's real in Pflugerville right now is public money. That's the strength, because a city project has a budget, a vote and a date on the calendar. It's also the risk, because residents are the ones who pay it back.

## What this means for your Pflugerville home value

If you own a home near Pecan Street or FM 685, a project like this can affect your home for years. A real downtown gives people a reason to want to live close by, and a new recreation center, hospital and trail connections add to that. I can't tell you by how much, and I haven't found a study that measures it.

It cuts both ways. The bond money gets repaid through taxes, so you get the new amenities and you help pay for them.

On price, I'll keep it simple. In recent closings, Pflugerville homes sold above Hutto and Manor and below Round Rock and Georgetown. That puts a city with a new downtown at a lower entry point than its bigger neighbors, which is a real selling point. Buyers also have room to negotiate right now, so pricing your home correctly matters more than it did a couple of years ago.
