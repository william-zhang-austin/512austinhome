Twenty major Austin development projects are being built right now, from an H-E-B going up on top of an old Buda landfill to Samsung's $37 billion-plus chip plant in Taylor. Some are finished and some are still holes in the ground. This is my countdown, 20 to 1, with the costs, the dates and what each one means if you own a home nearby, as of November 2026.

I go deeper on the seven biggest in [Austin's billion-dollar projects](/blog/austin-billion-dollar-projects/). We'll start south, in Buda.

## 20. H-E-B on the old Buda landfill

This one is on the southbound I-35 frontage road in Buda, just north of Main Street. The land sat empty for decades because it used to be a landfill. Now H-E-B is building a new store on it.

The cleanup alone is estimated at $30 million. So Buda, Hays County and the county's emergency services district agreed to put in up to $20.1 million over 30 years, paid back out of the taxes the store brings in. It's about 50 jobs to start. Construction should start in 2026, with no opening date yet.

## 19. Kyle Park

Kyle Park is about 100 acres along I-35 in Kyle, and it broke ground in February. It's a roughly $250 million project, called the biggest private investment in Kyle's history.

Tenants include Dick's Sporting Goods, TJ Maxx, Barnes & Noble and Raising Cane's, plus more than 400 apartments and more than 800 jobs. No opening date yet. For many Kyle homeowners, it's shopping they used to drive to Austin for.

## 18. Oak Hill Parkway

The Oak Hill Parkway is the "Y" where US 290 meets Highway 71. Construction started in 2021 and cost about $680 million. It's done, with a ribbon cutting in late September.

Traffic on 290 now goes past the Y without stopping at the lights, and there are about 15 miles of new paths and sidewalks. If you live in Oak Hill or out toward Dripping Springs, your drive just changed. Our [Austin commute guide](/blog/austin-commute-traffic-guide-2026/) covers how that plays into where people live.

## 17. The Confluence

The Confluence runs along Waller Creek from 4th Street down to Lady Bird Lake. It's about 13 acres and cost $91.5 million, with more than $62 million of that coming from the city.

There are 3 new bridges, a boardwalk over the creek and an overlook on the lake, plus about 200,000 new plants. It opened in June, later than planned, and it's free. For downtown owners, it makes the area feel more like a neighborhood.

## 16. Waterline and the Statesman site

Waterline sits at Red River and Cesar Chavez. It's 74 stories, more than 1,000 feet tall and the tallest building in Texas. It finished about 2 months early. The hotel opened in September and people are already moving into the apartments, where the cheapest one listed was just over $4,000 a month.

Now the part I want to be straight about. The old Statesman site on South Congress was approved in 2022 for about 1,400 homes in up to 6 buildings. In 2024, a judge blocked its tax deal, and as of this fall construction still hasn't started. Approved doesn't mean built.

## 15. Northline

Northline is in Leander, between 183A and US 183, right next to the Leander train station. It's 116 acres, and the developer says it'll be nearly a billion dollars when it's done, over about 10 years.

The city is moving offices and its council chambers into the first office building, due in 2027, and it's lending the project $4 million. There are already apartments and townhomes there. My [Leander development post](/blog/leander-tx-development-projects/) goes through more, and the [Leander seller page](/sell/leander/) covers selling there.

## 14. The Lake Travis deep-water intake

A water pipe makes this list because Cedar Park, Leander and Round Rock all get their water from Lake Travis, and they need it to keep coming when the lake drops.

So they dug 2 tunnels under the lake, each 11 feet wide, to a pump at the bottom of a shaft about 300 feet deep. It costs about $225 million and can move 145 million gallons a day. The tunnels are done, and the project should finish in 2027, running behind. Leander's old pipe failed 3 times in 3 years. Water is also the biggest fight on this list, as number 8 shows.

## 13. The District, Round Rock

The District is at Louie Henna and Greenlawn, just south of SH 45 and right next to Dell's headquarters. It's 65.5 acres and at least half a billion dollars.

That land next to Dell sat empty for years before work started in 2025. The first phase, 316 apartments over shops, is due in early 2028, and the whole thing builds out by 2039. More in [7 development projects in Round Rock](/blog/round-rock-development-projects/), and the [Round Rock seller page](/sell/round-rock/) covers listing there.

## 12. CedarView and Nebraska Furniture Mart

CedarView is 118 acres at 750 East New Hope Drive in Cedar Park, anchored by Nebraska Furniture Mart. The Austin Business Journal says that store is about 3 times the size of the IKEA in Round Rock. That's a furniture store the size of 3 IKEAs.

Plans include hotels and a city-owned convention center. The deal calls for at least $400 million in investment. Cedar Park offered $45 million in rebates, but they only pay if the stores hit their sales numbers. The store says it opens in spring 2027. If you're in Cedar Park, see the [Cedar Park seller page](/sell/cedar-park/).

## 11. Downtown East, Pflugerville

This is my town. Downtown East is at FM 685 and East Pecan Street, on 29 acres, with a new City Hall, a big recreation center called the Monarch with an indoor lazy river, and a plaza. It's more than $200 million.

City Hall opens to the public on January 4, and the recreation center opens later in January. The city borrowed to pay for it, and some neighbors are worried the new traffic light will back things up. Full breakdown in [Pflugerville's downtown development](/blog/pflugerville-downtown-development/), and the [Pflugerville seller page](/sell/pflugerville/) covers listing here.

## 10. UT Medical Center

The UT Medical Center is going on about 27 acres off MoPac and West Braker, near the Domain. It'll have a UT specialty hospital and an MD Anderson cancer hospital, with 300 to 500 beds.

In April, Michael and Susan Dell's foundation gave $750 million for the new campus. The plan is to break ground in late 2026 and open in 2030. The hospital was originally supposed to go where the Erwin Center stood. UT tore the arena down in 2024, then moved the hospital north in February, and there's no plan yet for the old site.

## 9. COTALAND

COTALAND is at Circuit of the Americas in southeast Austin. It's a 30-acre theme park with more than 30 rides, and it opened at the end of September. At opening, tickets ran about $40 to $75, and an annual pass started at $199.

It's one more thing pulling people toward the east side.

## 8. The data center rush

The biggest thing being built east of Austin isn't a ride. It's buildings full of computers. In Hutto, a data center campus off SH 79 promised at least $10 billion within 10 years when it got its tax deal in 2023. In Bastrop County, a 112-acre campus near FM 535 is already going up. In October, Taylor voted to annex a 664-acre campus with its own power plant, so the city could set the rules, and an overflow crowd showed up against it.

Here's the water fight. In San Marcos, a data center the developer valued at $1.5 billion was voted down 5 to 2 at about 2 in the morning, mostly over water. In June the council voted 4 to 3 to ban data centers citywide. In Hutto, another was pulled after neighbors pushed back. If you live near one, find out what's planned next door. Hutto owners can start at the [Hutto seller page](/sell/hutto/).

## 7. SpaceX and The Boring Company, Bastrop

Near Bastrop, SpaceX builds the dishes for Starlink, its satellite internet. Musk's Boring Company is headquartered in Bastrop County too, next to a small company town called Snailbrook that's still in its early days.

In March 2025, the state gave SpaceX a $17.3 million grant for an expansion worth more than $280 million, with more than 400 new jobs. This past May, Bastrop County backed another SpaceX expansion, even as some neighbors questioned the incentives. The biggest checks on this list are going east.

## 6. Tesla Giga Texas

Giga Texas is off SH 130 in southeast Travis County. It builds the Model Y, and now the Cybercab, Tesla's 2-seat robotaxi. On Tesla's last earnings call, Musk said the Cybercab fleet in Austin had grown about 10 times since it launched.

Musk posted this month that Cybertruck production in Austin will wind down around the middle of 2027, to make room for more Cybercabs. Tesla's next AI chips will come from number 1.

## 5. Austin Convention Center

The convention center is on the southeast corner of downtown. The city closed it in April 2025, tore it down and is building it back bigger, going from about 365,000 square feet of event space to about 620,000.

It costs about $1.6 billion. The city says it's paid for with hotel taxes and convention center revenue, not the general fund. The plan is to reopen in early 2029, so downtown goes about 4 years without it.

## 4. Journey With AUS

The airport expansion is called Journey With AUS, and it's more than $5 billion spread over more than a dozen projects. The new west gates cost $165 million and are open now.

At the end of September, the airport broke ground on Concourse M, 6 new gates you'll reach by shuttle bus, opening as early as 2028. The big piece, a new 26-gate concourse and a new terminal hall, stretches into the 2030s.

## 3. Project Connect light rail

Voters approved Project Connect in 2020, with 59% voting yes on a tax increase to pay for it. The first phase today is just under 10 miles with 15 street-level stations, at more than $8.2 billion. The downtown subway was cut in 2023 after its cost doubled. Construction is supposed to start in 2027, with first trains targeted for 2033.

The hard part is the money. About half is supposed to come from Washington, and none of that has been awarded. There's also a taxpayer lawsuit over how the bonds get paid back. It's on appeal and could hold up construction.

## 2. I-35 through central Austin

TxDOT is spending $4.5 billion to drop the main lanes below street level, as much as 60 feet down between Holly Street and Airport Boulevard. The upper decks are coming down too, and the work runs to about 2033.

When you sink a highway, you have to drain it. So TxDOT is building about 6.5 miles of drainage tunnels under the city, 22 feet wide, and plans to dig them with 2 giant tunnel boring machines built in Germany. The parks on top, the caps, are a different story: about 14 acres for about $600 million. The federal grant for them was canceled, and the city has committed $104 million, just for the foundations.

## 1. Samsung Taylor

Samsung is putting more than $37 billion into Texas, across its Taylor plant and its plant in Austin. The federal government added up to $4.745 billion in chips funding on top of that. Taylor is set to make Tesla's next AI chips on Samsung's 2-nanometer process.

Samsung announced Taylor in 2021 at $17 billion, and it was supposed to open in 2024. Samsung now says the first plant should start operating by the end of 2026, with mass production for customers in 2027. A second plant is set to start construction around the end of 2026, with production targeted for 2030.

## What this means for your Austin home value

Projects like these show up in what a home is worth. Sometimes that helps and sometimes it doesn't, and a lot of it gets paid back through taxes (see the [Austin property tax guide](/blog/austin-property-tax-guide-2026/)).

Notice the east side. Samsung, Tesla, the data centers and SpaceX are all east of Austin. In recent closings, homes with an Austin address were roughly flat from a year earlier, while most suburbs sold lower, and the east-side suburbs sit among the more affordable parts of the metro. That doesn't tell you what prices will do next. It's the part of the map I'd keep an eye on, and I wrote more on why suburb prices keep slipping in [why Austin suburb home prices are dropping](/blog/why-austin-suburb-home-prices-are-dropping/).

In Austin itself, most recent closings were below original asking price. So these projects don't set your price on their own. Your street, your house and your timing do.

Timing matters too. The stretch between approved and open is often when a project starts showing up in what buyers will pay. Once it's built, it's usually already in the number. Pflugerville's City Hall opens January 4, the Cedar Park store opens in 2027, Concourse M in 2028, the convention center in 2029, the hospital in 2030 and the first trains in 2033.
