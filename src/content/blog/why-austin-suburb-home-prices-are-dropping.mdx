Austin suburb home prices are dropping, and the gap between the city and the towns around it is easy to see. Unlock MLS's September 2026 report put the median sale price inside the city of Austin at $570,958, up 4.2% from a year earlier. Out in Manor, Kyle and Hutto, resale homes sold for noticeably less per square foot than they did a year ago.

It's the same metro, but right now it works like two markets. If you own a home 20 or 30 minutes from downtown, you've probably felt it.

I'm William Zhang, an Austin real estate agent with eXp Realty. I live in Pflugerville, I've been in the Austin area more than 12 years, and I list homes across the metro. Before you panic, you should know why this is happening. The main reason isn't people leaving. Here's what's behind it, and what it means for your house. ## It isn't people leaving

Start with the obvious question. Are people moving out of Austin? The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates put the city of Austin at 1,002,632 residents, so it passed a million people in 2025. Austin did lose some jobs this fall, according to the Dallas Fed, but its figures put unemployment at 3.6%.

Prices are also still well below where they were. On Zillow's measure, the Austin area is about 27% below its 2022 peak, per ResiClub's analysis of Zillow's home value index, and that's one of the biggest drops of any large market in the country. The city holding steady is not the same as Austin recovering.

So if people are still here, why are prices falling out in the suburbs? A big part of the answer is the house down the road.

## The builder down the road

Drive out Highway 29 in Liberty Hill and you'll reach Santa Rita Ranch. It's about 3,800 acres with about 10,000 homes planned and more than 4,200 already built. According to a local news report citing RCLCO data, it sold 483 homes in 2025, ranking among the top-selling communities in the country.

Head up I-35 past Georgetown to Jarrell and you'll find Sonterra, which sold about 400 homes in 2025 with builders like D.R. Horton, Lennar and KB Home.

Communities like these are why a brand-new house can sell for less than yours. There are so many of them. Zillow's July 2026 medians put new homes in the Austin area at about $184 per square foot, against $228 for existing homes. That's roughly 19% less, and HousingWire reported it as the biggest gap among the large metros Zillow looked at. Nationally the two were almost even, $205 and $212.

Think about what that means. A new house with a builder warranty and a new roof is selling for less per square foot than the homes around it.

The cuts didn't stop there. A local brokerage analysis in March found that roughly two out of three new-construction listings in Hutto, Georgetown, Liberty Hill and Kyle had already dropped their price. Many builders would rather not lower the sticker price at all, because a lower price becomes a recorded sale. Instead they buy down the buyer's interest rate. Some entry-level communities have advertised rates as low as 3.25% for the first year or so, according to Spyglass Realty. That's the deal a buyer compares your house to. (If you're curious how builders structure these, see our page on [builder incentives](/incentives/), and our [new construction](/new-construction/) overview.)

Zillow senior economist Kara Ng put it simply: "There are just more homes available, so the market is slower for a seller."

It isn't a perfect rule. Some towns with a lot of new homes held up better than their neighbors, so your neighborhood matters more than your town. But if you're selling a ten or fifteen year old house near a new subdivision, your competition isn't the neighbor. It's the builder.

## The apartment wave

Builders weren't the only ones building. The bigger wave was apartments.

In 2024, the Austin area finished more than 31,000 apartments in one year, the most ever. A normal year before the pandemic was roughly 11,000 to 12,000, according to the Austin Apartment Association as quoted by KXAN. That's close to three normal years of apartments arriving at once.

You've probably seen the leasing banners. This summer, 73% of apartments in the metro were offering some kind of concession, per MRI figures reported by KXAN. One renter got 10 weeks free plus a $1,000 gift card. MRI also showed average asking rents down about 16% from the 2022 peak.

The Texas A&M Real Estate Center described the imbalance this way: Austin holds about 22% of the extra apartments in Texas but only about 13% of all apartments in the state. During the boom, four apartments were started here for every new household.

So why does an apartment downtown matter to a house in Hutto? When rent is this cheap and you can get weeks for free, a lot of people who would normally buy a first home keep renting another year. That's my read, not a statistic. But every renter who waits is one less buyer for a starter home.

## Rates went the wrong way

Then there's the part nobody around here controls. In early November 2026, Freddie Mac's average 30-year mortgage rate was 7.18%. That was down three weeks in a row, but a year earlier it was 6.22%, and in early October it touched about 7.4%, the highest in almost three years, as reported by the Associated Press.

On a $400,000 loan, going from 6.22% to 7.18% adds about $250 to the monthly payment. Buyers slowed down. Unlock MLS reported that metro home sales in September were down almost 15% from a year earlier (2,223 closed sales) and tied the drop to mortgage rate sensitivity.

I can't tell you when rates come down. Nobody can.

## What didn't get built downtown

Here's a sign of where the building wave is heading. At Fifth and Red River, on the old Carmelo's restaurant site, a developer planned a 37-story apartment tower, proposed in 2021 near the top of the boom. In early 2025 they dropped the plan and put the land up for sale.

A few blocks from the Capitol, at 14th and Guadalupe, another developer planned a 35-story condo tower about 400 feet tall. Last December they paused the sales launch and the groundbreaking, saying they were waiting for "more stable market conditions." As far as I can find, it hasn't restarted. For more on what is being built, see my breakdown of [billion-dollar projects across the Austin metro](/blog/austin-billion-dollar-projects/).

When developers stop starting projects, the extra apartments slowly get used up.

## The builders already blinked

Out in the suburbs, builders are pulling back too. Zillow reported that Austin's residential permits fell 25.3% in the 12 months through July 2026, the steepest drop among major markets. Ng said builders are pulling back "especially in the places they'd been building the most."

Apartments are slowing even faster. Apartment approvals across the metro are down 79% from five years ago, according to the Austin Business Journal as reported by CRE Daily. KXAN reported that only about 10,000 new apartments are expected to finish in 2026, roughly a third of the 2024 wave. And renters are now filling apartments faster than new ones open: about 20,000 leased over a year, against about 12,500 finished, based on figures from Transwestern and Matthews.

Two cautions. Nobody can promise if or when suburban prices steady, and Texas A&M figures the surplus apartments alone would take about three years to absorb even with zero new building. I also don't know anyone with a trustworthy call on the bottom. I wrote more about that in [where Austin home prices hit bottom first](/blog/austin-home-prices-bottom/).

But demand hasn't gone anywhere. As Ng put it, "the housing shortage that drove the building boom is still very much intact."

## What this means for your home value in the Austin suburbs

If you bought before the pandemic, you're most likely still well ahead. If you bought at the very top in spring 2022, it's a different story, and the numbers on your street may not match what you paid. In recent closings, a large share of homes in towns like Hutto, Manor and Pflugerville sold below their original list price.

The mistake I see most is pricing off a neighbor's sale from two years ago. That number is gone. What sells now is a price that makes sense next to the new house down the road and next to the other resale homes on your street. Homes priced right are still selling. Homes priced like it's 2022 sit, get cut, and sit again. For realistic timing, see [how long it takes to sell a home in Austin](/blog/how-long-to-sell-a-home-in-austin/).

It also cuts both ways. A new subdivision near you brings new shops, new roads and new neighbors. It also brings competition the day you list.

If you're thinking about selling in the next year or two, don't watch the national news. Watch the subdivision next to yours: how many new homes are still sitting there and what the builder is offering. That's what your price gets compared to.
