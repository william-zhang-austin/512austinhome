Georgetown TX development projects are moving fast this year, and the one that started it all is a $272.4 million water plant. The city cut the ribbon on it in August 2026. It's the South Lake Water Treatment Plant, and city leaders say it doubles how much water Georgetown can treat.

That plant has a backstory. In July 2023 the city told every water customer to turn off their sprinklers because the plants were running close to their limit. Two months earlier, the Census had named Georgetown the fastest-growing city of its size in America.

## 1. The South Lake Water Treatment Plant

The project has three parts: a new intake out in Lake Georgetown, a brand new plant to clean the water, and a 7.5-mile pipe that carries it into town. It can treat 44 million gallons a day. The city started building in 2022, in two phases, and finished this summer. The mayor called it the largest capital improvement project in the history of the city.

Remember that summer in 2023. Sprinklers were banned for everyone on city water for four days in mid-July. On the west side, anyone living west of D.B. Wood Road or southwest of Williams Drive, in places like Parmer Ranch and Santa Rita, stayed under the strictest rules until the end of September.

Here's the part to be straight about. The plant was paid for with water revenue and with fees charged to new development. Water revenue means your water bill, so you help pay for it every month.

Why care about a water plant? A city can't approve new homes, new schools or new factories if it can't get water to them. Water comes first, and the other six projects on this list follow it.

There's more coming. The city has a $10.6 million water line planned in the northwest, along County Road 255 toward Ronald Reagan Boulevard, meant to serve about 25,000 customers. The west side, where the 2023 rules lasted longest, is also where several of the big projects below are going up.

## 2. Georgetown ISD's new high school

Georgetown ISD is building a brand new high school on D.B. Wood Road, the same road the city used to draw the line on the 2023 water ban. The bond set aside about $356 million for it. Crews broke ground in October 2025, and it's set to open in the fall of 2028 with room for 2,200 students.

It's part of a $649.5 million bond voters passed in May 2024, the largest in the history of the district. The same bond also pays for a fifth middle school and a twelfth elementary school.

You don't need kids in school for this to matter. When a new high school opens, school zones often get redrawn, and a lot of families shop for a home by school zone first. If you live on the west side, it's worth watching how zones shake out.

Georgetown voters don't say yes to everything, though. In 2021 they were asked for a $23.6 million district swim center and turned it down. That pool idea comes back in project 7.

## 3. Heirloom

Heirloom is the biggest piece of land on this list: 620 acres at the northwest corner of Ronald Reagan Boulevard and County Road 248, well past D.B. Wood Road on the west side of town. The plan calls for about 3,000 homes, and early approvals capped it at 3,600. There's land for shops, about 60 acres of parks and 120 acres saved for a future school.

It sits in a fast-growing corridor. As of December 2025, more than 8,500 homes had been approved along Ronald Reagan, and the H-E-B at Parmer Ranch opened in September 2025.

Paying for roads, water and sewer on open land is the big question. Heirloom needs $100.4 million of it, handled through a Public Improvement District, or PID. In plain English, it gets paid back through a charge on the properties inside Heirloom. If you want the full picture of how that kind of charge works, I wrote about [MUDs and PIDs](/blog/austin-mud-pid-explained/).

This one is still mostly a plan. The city was still working through the financing this fall. The first homes are planned to start around $400,000, and the first phase is expected to wrap up in 2027. The whole build is supposed to take about 10 years.

For nearby homes, 3,000 new houses means more competition for resale. It also brings the stores and restaurants that tend to follow new rooftops. If you're on the west side, you may feel this before anyone else. If you're comparing [new construction in Georgetown](/new-construction/georgetown/), this is the community to know about.

## 4. Pegatron and Compal

Homes need jobs, and these two came from Taiwan. Pegatron, a big electronics maker, owns a 168,784 square foot building on Blue Springs Boulevard near the Southeast Inner Loop. It's the company's first manufacturing site in the United States. Pegatron agreed to invest at least $35 million and hire at least 100 people within three years. Under its deal, it had to start building by March 31, 2026.

Compal, another Taiwanese electronics company, is leasing a building on Aviation Drive, off I-35. Its numbers look almost the same: $35 million, at least 100 jobs, and a goal of opening in February 2027. Between the two that's at least 200 jobs. Both got 10-year tax breaks from the city and the county, and those breaks are tied to hitting their numbers.

To get people to that side of town, the city is widening the Southeast Inner Loop. That's 2.9 miles from Highway 29 to FM 1460, four lanes, divided. Two things you should know. The 2021 bond set aside about $32 million for part of this road, and today the project is $70.6 million. As of this summer, crews were still moving utilities and it hadn't gone out to bid.

For homes on the east side, a few hundred new jobs means buyers who want to live close to work. Treat the opening dates as targets until the buildings are running.

## 5. Baer Manufacturing at CrossPoint

On the north side, between I-35 and Highway 195, is a 224-acre business park called CrossPoint. The newest building going up there is 606,000 square feet, more than ten football fields under one roof. It's for Baer Manufacturing, which makes prefab electrical equipment that is built in a factory and shipped to job sites.

Construction was set to start in March 2026, with a move-in planned for mid-2027 and between 200 and 250 jobs.

Why care about a factory on I-35? Because 200 jobs or more means 200 paychecks, and many of those workers will want to live nearby or shop and eat in Georgetown every day. For homes on the north side, that's more demand coming your way. Note that this one is a plan on a schedule, not a finished building.

The building right next door at CrossPoint has a very different story, and I'll get to it after project 7.

## 6. SU 560 and the city's Customer Service Center

Southwestern University owns 560 acres just east of its campus, around University Avenue and Smith Creek Road, a short drive from the Square. The first building out there will be the city's new Customer Service Center: about $49 million, 75,567 square feet, set to open in early 2028, paid for with city bonds.

That's just the start. The plan for the first phase alone includes offices, shops, 150 apartments, a hotel and conference center, and 35 acres of parks. The roads and utilities for that phase run about $16 million.

The thinking is simple. Once the city is out there every day, shops and restaurants have customers. For homes on the east side of downtown, this could change how that whole side of town looks over the years. Most of it is still planned rather than built.

## 7. The YMCA on Williams Drive

At 6200 Williams Drive, Georgetown is getting its first full-service YMCA. Ground was broken on May 29, 2026. It's 55,000 square feet, with the main building set to open in January 2028 and the outdoor pool in May 2028.

Remember the swim center voters turned down in 2021? This isn't the same project, but Georgetown families are getting a pool through a different door. The city put in $10 million from its 2023 bond, and the YMCA and local donors cover the rest. It was first planned as a $23 million project. The YMCA's CEO now puts it at about $38 million.

While you're on Williams Drive, watch for more road work. The city is turning the center turn lane into a landscaped median from I-35 to Serenada, close to $20 million, and as of August the bids were still being reviewed. Shell Road is being widened from Williams Drive to Sycamore for about $18 to $19 million, with a finish planned for the end of 2027. For the west side, that's a pool, a gym and better roads close to home.

## What didn't happen: Onx Homes and CrossPoint

A plan is not a promise, and Georgetown has an example. A homebuilder called Onx Homes leased that whole building at CrossPoint, all 204,000 square feet, to build homes in a factory and set them in place. In February 2026, the Austin Business Journal reported that Onx had quietly moved out.

Onx also had a half-built, 277-acre neighborhood off County Road 289 in Williamson County, called On Rocking Wilco. A neighbor said it had looked empty for months, and by late spring the land was headed to a foreclosure auction. I couldn't find a statement from Onx or the auction result.

So in the same business park, one company is moving into a brand new 606,000 square foot building while another left a 204,000 square foot one. If a project near you stalls, a half-built lot can sit next to finished homes for a long time. That's why I look at what's actually happening on the ground and not just the announcements.

## What this means for your Georgetown home value

I can't tell you what any of this does to your price, and anyone who gives you a number is guessing. Here's what I can say.

New schools, jobs, parks and a YMCA tend to make an area more attractive to buyers. But more people means more traffic, and a lot of this gets paid back through your taxes and your bills. If you want the tax side explained, start with my [Austin property tax guide](/blog/austin-property-tax-guide-2026/). You get the new things, and you help pay for them.

On pricing, I'm keeping this qualitative. In recent closings, Georgetown sat in the middle of its neighbors on price, between Round Rock and Cedar Park, and most homes sold for less than their original asking price. Buyers have choices, so how you price matters. That's true across the area, and it's the same story I've written up in [how long it takes to sell a home in Austin](/blog/how-long-to-sell-a-home-in-austin/).

Timing is the other piece. Here are the dates as of November 2026:

- The water plant is finished (August 2026).
- Compal is aiming to open in February 2027.
- Baer and Heirloom's first phase are planned for 2027.
- The new high school opens in fall 2028, and the YMCA in 2028.

If you're on the west side and Heirloom's new homes start showing up next door, you'll want your real number before you list, not after. A lot of owners wait until a project opens to ask what it did for their home. The better time to ask is before.
