At least half a billion dollars is going into 65 acres next to Dell's headquarters in Round Rock. It's called The District, and it's the biggest of the Round Rock development projects I'm tracking. It won't be fully built until the late 2030s. Round Rock has 143,075 people today, according to the city's estimate, and it's still building in almost every direction.

Below are seven projects (plus one failed bond election) and what each means for homes nearby, with status as of November 2026. Some are open, some are under construction, and some are still plans.

## The District at Louis Henna and Greenlawn

The District covers about 65 acres at Louis Henna Boulevard and Greenlawn Boulevard. If you've driven to Dell's campus, you've driven past it.

The land used to belong to Dell. Dell sold it to a developer called Mark IV Capital in 2016, and then it sat for nearly a decade. Ground was finally broken in March 2025.

The first phase is Origin at The District: 316 apartments, about 23,000 square feet of ground-floor shops, and a food and drink plaza made up of six buildings. Mark IV Capital got an $86 million construction loan for it in early 2026. The total project is at least half a billion dollars once it's all built. Reports vary on the exact figure, and one 2026 trade report put it above $1 billion, so I use the lower number as a floor. The city is also putting in millions for roads and utilities.

Here's the honest part. When they broke ground, the first phase was supposed to finish in early 2027. It's now early 2028. It slipped about a year before a single apartment opened.

For nearby homes, a place where people can walk to dinner can make the surrounding streets more wanted. I won't put a number on it. The city's share comes out of local taxes, and more people means more traffic, so it cuts both ways. If you want to see how taxes work in this area, I wrote a [property tax guide](/blog/austin-property-tax-guide-2026/).

## Ascension Seton Williamson's new patient tower

This one is already open. The new patient tower at Ascension Seton Williamson opened on September 1, 2026. It cost $230 million, stands six stories and covers 216,000 square feet.

It opened with 96 beds and was built with room to grow to 160. It also added two new operating rooms, a cath lab, an MRI and a bigger emergency room. So it was built for the Round Rock that's expected to arrive, not just the one we have now.

A hospital matters to homeowners in two ways. Families with kids or older parents care about how close it is, and buyers ask about that drive time. And hospitals hire a lot of people. Nurses, doctors and techs have jobs that tend to hold up when the economy gets shaky, and people with steady jobs are the ones who buy homes and stay in them.

## The Switch data centers near Mays Street and Dell Way

Just east of South Mays Street and north of Dell Way, again next to Dell, sits a data center company called Switch. Most people in Round Rock have driven past it without noticing.

In December 2022, the city council voted 7 to 0 on an economic development deal with Switch. The company agreed to build at least 150,000 square feet, invest $80 million by the end of 2026, and create 15 jobs. A second building of about 72,600 square feet is planned, with a finish in mid-2028. As of November 2026, I haven't confirmed that construction on the second building has started.

The jobs number is the surprising part. In the city's own report, Switch had added 1 of its 15 promised jobs as of September 2025. So why would a city want a building with almost no people in it? Taxes. The same city summary lists about $18.6 million in value for the property.

For homes near Mays and Dell Way, a data center is a quiet neighbor. It doesn't bring new traffic or fill the schools.

## The Lake Travis deep water intake

Out on Lake Travis, Round Rock is working with Leander and Cedar Park on a deep water intake. The raw-water contract is about $225 million, and the work is expected to finish in 2027. Three cities sharing one project shows how much each is betting on growth.

The idea is simple. These cities pull their water from the lake, and in a bad drought the lake drops. A deeper intake means the pipe still reaches water when levels are low.

Water also decides how much a city can keep building. Every apartment at The District and every new home on the north side needs it. For your home, that means the demand behind Round Rock prices is less likely to hit a water limit.

## Old Settlers Park and the 2023 bond

In 2023, Round Rock voters passed a $274 million bond, the largest in the city's history. About $147 million of it is going to one park, Old Settlers Park.

That money pays for a bigger Multipurpose Complex, the Lakeview Pavilion and Rock'N River. The city expects the park expansion to wrap up in 2027. The same bond put $44 million toward public safety, which covers a training center and two new fire stations.

Drive past the park on a Saturday and look at the parking lot. Parks like this pull families in, and families are who buy three and four bedroom homes. A fire station down the road is something most people don't think about until they need it.

## The $200 million road program

Round Rock has had over $200 million in road work underway or on the way at the same time. Red Bud Lane North, Gattis School Road, County Road 112 and Greenlawn are all on that list, and most of it won't wrap up until 2027.

Kenney Fort Boulevard is also planned to be extended to University Boulevard. Regional grant money covers about $37 million of that, and I couldn't confirm a construction start date.

I'll be straight about the downside. Before these roads get better, they get worse. If you drive Red Bud or Gattis, you already know. Expect a few more years of orange cones. Buyers will count the minutes those cones add to a commute, so it's worth counting them yourself.

The payoff is that a road like Kenney Fort opens up land and cuts drive times. Neighborhoods along a road that makes it easier to cross town tend to get easier to sell.

## Starling on the Round Rock and Georgetown line

Up at Rockride Lane and County Road 110, a builder called Risewell Homes is planning a community called Starling. It covers about 230 acres and 900 single-family homes. An August 2026 Hoodline report said construction was slated to start this fall, with first homes expected in 2027 and full buildout taking about 10 years. This is a single-source report, so treat the timing as planned.

That's a small town showing up on the north side. If you live up there, drive County Road 110 this weekend and look at the fields that will turn into streets.

There's a catch if you live nearby. New homes compete with resale. If you're selling a five-year-old house a mile away, your buyer can also walk into a brand new one with a builder warranty, so you have to price against that. You can see the new-home side of that comparison on my [Round Rock new construction page](/new-construction/round-rock/). It works the other way too: 900 new households bring more demand for shops and restaurants up there, and that helps the whole area.

Starling is only one of several new communities in the north metro. My post on [Georgetown development projects](/blog/georgetown-tx-development-projects/) covers what's coming just across the line.

## The 2017 school bond that failed

Round Rock hasn't always said yes to growth. In May 2017, the Round Rock school district asked voters for $572 million. It would have paid for a sixth high school, a career and technical high school, and arts and athletic upgrades. Voters turned down all three ballot measures.

That was the school district, not the city, but it tells you something. Developers can build all the apartments and homes they want, and schools only get built when voters approve them. When you hear about 900 new homes or 316 new apartments, the real question is whether the schools keep up. If you own here, pay attention to school bond elections.

## What this means for your home value in Round Rock

I looked at recent Round Rock closings against the neighboring cities. Round Rock sold for less than Georgetown, Leander and Cedar Park, and for more than Pflugerville and Hutto. The gap with Cedar Park was big, and buyers weigh that gap against your price.

What sellers should notice is that most Round Rock homes in recent closings sold for less than their original asking price. Buyers are negotiating.

So the projects above help, and they also cut both ways. A hospital and a park can pull buyers in. Roadwork, new subdivisions and tax-funded projects can push the other way. Nobody can say what any of this will do to a price. Homes near The District and Starling are closest to the change, and in a market where buyers negotiate, pricing right matters most.

My advice is to find out what your home is worth before projects like these are finished and everyone can see them. Once a project is built, buyers usually already know about it. The water intake is expected in 2027 and the first phase of The District in early 2028.
