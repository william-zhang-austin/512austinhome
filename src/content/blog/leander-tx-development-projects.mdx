Leander has a new downtown going up next to its train station: Northline, a mixed-use project worth close to $1 billion on 116 acres. The city is moving its own City Hall into it. That's the biggest of the Leander TX development projects I'm tracking, and there are six more worth knowing about, from a new water intake under Lake Travis to a $55 million youth sports complex.

First, the pace of Leander Texas growth. In 2019 the Census named Leander the fastest-growing large city in America. About 59,000 people lived here in 2020. The latest Census estimate is more than 91,000, which is over 50% bigger in five years.

## Northline: Leander's new downtown

Northline sits between 183A and US 183, just north of the Leander train station and the Austin Community College San Gabriel campus. Leander has a train station, a college and a toll road, but it never really had a downtown. This is the plan to fix that.

If you've driven past, you've seen the start of it. There are already apartments and townhomes, and some public space is open. The full plan mixes homes, shops, offices and places to hang out. It's still years from finished, so this is a long build.

Here's the part that surprised me. This February, the city council approved a deal for a new 95,000 square foot office building at Northline, set to be done in 2027. The city will lease about 40,000 square feet of it for 10 years. That space holds city offices, council chambers and a temporary Leander City Hall.

The city also agreed to a $4 million loan to the developer to help get that building built. It has an option to buy land nearby for a permanent City Hall later, and it's looking at a new use for the old City Hall in Old Town. A Sprouts and something called the Market District are coming to Northline too. I cover both below.

For homes nearby, a real downtown gives Leander a place to go that isn't Austin. That's a real draw for buyers over time. It's also a long timeline, so I wouldn't price a home today as if it's finished.

## The deep-water intake under Lake Travis

Leander gets much of its water from Lake Travis. It comes through a pipe from the lake to a plant the city shares with Cedar Park and Round Rock. Between 2020 and 2023, that pipe failed three times in three years.

If you lived here in September 2022, you remember it. Leander banned all outdoor watering and closed the splash pads for about a month. In February 2024 it happened again: no outdoor watering for about seven weeks while crews replaced 1,418 feet of pipe under the lake. When that line goes down, Leander's water plant drops from about 24 million gallons a day to just 9.

The regional water authority is building a fix, a brand new intake deep under Lake Travis. As of December 2025, all the tunnels under the lake were done. When it's finished it can pull 145 million gallons a day, and it replaces a floating intake that has trouble when the lake gets low. A roughly $98 million plant expansion started in December 2025, and the whole program was estimated at about $293 million.

I'll be straight about the schedule. The intake was scheduled for early 2027, and as of December 2025 the contractor was four to six months behind.

Why care about a pipe at the bottom of a lake? A city that can't promise water can't grow. For homes nearby, steady water is what lets the rest of this list get built.

## Hero Way, Ronald Reagan and 183A

The one that's already done: 183A. It opened all the way past Highway 29 last year. Most of it opened in April, and the last piece under Highway 29 opened in May. It's a $259 million extension that gives you about 16 miles of non-stop driving from Liberty Hill down to northwest Austin.

Now for what's still under construction. Williamson County is turning Hero Way (RM 2243) into a highway with frontage roads, from 183A out to the Southwest Bypass. It's $132 million. Work started in late January 2026 and is set to wrap up in early 2028.

On Ronald Reagan Boulevard, the county is widening about 7 miles from FM 3405 up to Highway 29. That's $52.4 million, with a finish planned for summer 2027. Both projects are paid for by the county road bond voters passed in 2019. The next wave is already lined up: Bagdad Road, Freedom River Parkway and County Road 175 are all planned to start in 2027.

A shorter drive to work means more buyers who can picture living here. You'll sit in the cones first, though. If commute time is part of your decision, my [Austin commute and traffic guide](/blog/austin-commute-traffic-guide-2026/) covers the corridors.

## CenTex Sports Complex at 204 Heritage Grove Road

About a mile from Northline, there's a 40-acre site at 204 Heritage Grove Road, right off 183A. CenTex Sports, a Central Texas youth sports company, wants to buy it and build a youth sports complex.

Phase one is $55 million. It includes an indoor building of more than 150,000 square feet, a covered track, four beach volleyball courts and soccer fields. A second phase would add baseball and softball fields. Under the deal terms, phase one has to start by the end of 2027.

There's a catch. What the council approved on September 17 was a letter of intent, not a final deal. As of early November 2026, the real agreement still hadn't been signed.

You don't need kids in sports for this to matter. Tournament weekends bring families from out of town who need hotel rooms and restaurants and who shop while they're here. For homes nearby, that could mean busy Saturdays on 183A. It's also the kind of amenity people look for when they pick a town.

## Leander Union, Sprouts and the Market District

Some of the retail is already here. Leander Union is a 13-acre center with 10 buildings and 155,000 square feet, east of Crystal Falls Parkway and 183A. It had its ribbon cutting on October 21, and Westlake Dermatology was the first business to open there, back in September.

At Northline, a 25,000 square foot Sprouts is planned, a $5.8 million build expected to open in 2027. A grocery store matters more than it sounds. It's often the first thing other shops want to see before they move in next door.

Then there's the Market District: 30,000 square feet of shops and restaurants at Northline, with a focus on Asian and local businesses. There's no opening date yet. If you've ever driven to north Austin for a great meal, this one could save you the trip.

Shops tend to follow new homes, and once shops show up, the homes near them get easier to sell. If you're curious which new communities are going in around this, I keep a list of [new construction homes in Leander](/new-construction/leander/).

## Ada Mae Faubion School for Early Childhood

At Halsey Road and San Gabriel Parkway, Leander ISD has a new school, its first just for early childhood. It serves 3 and 4 year olds and is named the Ada Mae Faubion School for Early Childhood. It was set to open in fall 2026.

The building permit put it at more than $48 million. The district set aside more than $60 million for it from the bond voters passed in 2023. That bond was $762.8 million, and every part of it passed.

What's next? A district committee has put together a plan for another bond, mostly for repairs and updates rather than new schools. The district says it could come in 2027.

If your kids are grown, this still matters. A lot of buyers shop for a home by school district first, and a district that keeps investing helps every home in it. The other side is that bonds are paid back through property taxes, which I break down in my [Austin property tax guide](/blog/austin-property-tax-guide-2026/).

## The recreation center and the city's 70-project plan

The city has put a price tag of about $47.5 million on a new recreation center. It's part of a 70-project plan the city adopted on June 18, 2026. Just under $355 million of that plan is funded through 2030. The plan also includes a $13.1 million fire station near Ronald Reagan and San Gabriel Parkway.

The city is still looking for a site for the rec center, and the Pat Bryson Building is one option. Construction isn't expected until around 2029 or 2030. Another $329.7 million of projects in the same plan still need funding.

For homes nearby, a plan like this shows where the city expects families to keep moving. A fire station and a rec center on the west side are a pretty clear signal.

## What didn't happen: Leander Springs

This isn't the first $1 billion project Leander has been promised. Leander Springs was a $1 billion plan on 78 acres at Hero Way and 183A, with a 4-acre lagoon in the middle. The city approved it in 2021.

The deal said the developers had to finish 35,000 square feet of shops and the lagoon by the end of 2023. They didn't. On March 18, 2024, the city sent notice ending the agreement. The developers came back with a new plan, and in August 2025 the council said no. They tried again this year with fewer apartments, and in August 2026 the council turned it down again. For now, the lagoon can't be built.

It's not the only one. St. David's planned a hospital in Leander that was supposed to open in 2025. It's still not built.

Approved isn't built. If a big plan near your home is only approved, don't count on it yet. That goes for the CenTex complex too, until the final agreement is signed.

More people also means more traffic, and a lot of this gets paid back through your taxes and your water bill. You get the new stuff, and you help pay for it.
